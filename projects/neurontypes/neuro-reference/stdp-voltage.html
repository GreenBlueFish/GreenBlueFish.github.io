STDP 04
Voltage-Dependent STDP (Clopath Rule)

Plasticity gated by the postsynaptic membrane voltage rather than spike occurrence alone. Three voltage traces drive LTP and LTD through rectified thresholds. More biologically realistic — NMDA receptors are voltage-gated coincidence detectors, so postsynaptic voltage directly controls Ca²⁺ influx and plasticity.

Learning Rule

Clopath LTP & LTD
LTP: dw/dt = ALTP · [ū₋ − θ₋]₊ · [u − θ₊]₊ · x̄
LTD: dw/dt = −ALTD · ū₊ · [ū₋ − θ₋]₊ · x
Voltage Traces
τ₋ · dū₋/dt = −ū₋ + u(t)
τ₊ · dū₊/dt = −ū₊ + u(t)

Biological Interpretation

Why voltage rather than spikes?
LTP requires: (1) presynaptic glutamate (captured by x̄) AND (2) sufficient postsynaptic depolarization to unblock NMDA receptors (captured by [u − θ₊]₊) AND (3) the postsynaptic neuron was recently active (captured by [ū₋ − θ₋]₊). All three conditions mirror the biophysics of NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx.

LTD requires: presynaptic activity (x) during periods of low postsynaptic voltage following prior activity (ū₋ > θ₋ but u < θ₊). This corresponds to homosynaptic LTD from subthreshold postsynaptic depolarization — a well-documented biological phenomenon.

Advantages

  • Most biologically realistic STDP formulation — voltage is the actual NMDA gate
  • Reproduces STDP, BCM, and rate-dependent LTP/LTD in one framework
  • Predicts STDP reversal at different holding potentials — confirmed experimentally
  • Works with voltage-based neuron models (EIF, HH, AdEx) without modification

Limitations

  • Complex: many parameters (θ₋, θ₊, τ₋, τ₊, A_LTP, A_LTD, τ_x)
  • Requires continuous access to postsynaptic voltage — harder on event-based hardware
  • Voltage traces must be integrated with care to avoid numerical instability
  • Fitting requires combined voltage-clamp and plasticity data
