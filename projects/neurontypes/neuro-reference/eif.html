MODEL 02
Exponential Integrate-and-Fire

The EIF extends the LIF by replacing the hard threshold with an exponential term that smoothly models sodium channel activation near the spike onset. This produces more realistic spike initiation dynamics and slightly improved timing precision.

ODEs1
Spike PrecisionHigh
Dendritic EffectsNone
Computational CostLow
Biological FidelityMedium

Governing Equation

Membrane Voltage
Cm · dV/dt = −gL(V − EL) + gL · δT · exp((V − VT)/δT) + I(t)
SymbolDescriptionTypical
CmMembrane capacitance200 pF
gLLeak conductance — sets passive decay rate10 nS
ELLeak reversal potential (≈ resting potential)−70 mV
δTSlope factor — controls sharpness of spike initiation0.5–2 mV
VTEffective threshold — where exponential term dominates−55 mV
I(t)Total input currentpA
The exponential term: When V ≪ VT, exp((V−VT)/δT) ≈ 0 and the model behaves like a LIF. As V approaches VT, the term explodes, driving an autocatalytic spike — mimicking Na⁺ channel opening.

Reset Mechanism

Numerical Cutoff Reset
if V ≥ Vpeak (e.g. +20 mV) : emit spike, V ← Vreset
SymbolDescriptionTypical
VpeakNumerical spike cutoff — exponential would diverge to +∞+20 mV
VresetPost-spike reset potential−65 mV

Advantages

  • Realistic spike initiation — matches cortical neuron voltage-clamp data
  • Smooth threshold removes ambiguity from noisy voltage traces
  • Natural for voltage-dependent STDP (Clopath rule)
  • Still one ODE — very low computational cost

Limitations

  • Requires smaller time step near threshold than LIF
  • No adaptation — still cannot reproduce spike frequency adaptation
  • Extra parameters δ_T and V_T must be fit
  • No dendritic or subthreshold resonance dynamics
