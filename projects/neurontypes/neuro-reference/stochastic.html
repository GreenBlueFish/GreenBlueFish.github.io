MODEL 10
Stochastic Integrate-and-Fire

Extends the LIF with noise to capture trial-to-trial variability, channel noise, and synaptic fluctuations. Two formulations: additive voltage noise (Langevin) or an escape-rate hazard function (SRM₀). Connects STDP to Bayesian inference — the neuron can be shown to approximate maximum likelihood estimation of its inputs.

ODEs1+noise
Spike PrecisionModerate
Dendritic EffectsNone
Computational CostLow

Governing Equations — Two Formulations

Langevin (Noise-Voltage)
τm · dV/dt = −(V − Vrest) + R·I(t) + σ · ξ(t)
Escape Noise (Hazard Function)
ρ(t) = ρ₀ · exp((V(t) − Vthresh) / δu)

Reset Mechanism

In the Langevin formulation, reset is identical to LIF — V jumps to Vreset upon a threshold crossing, which now occurs stochastically due to noise.

In the escape noise formulation, a spike is generated at random time with probability ρ(t)·dt per timestep. Upon spike generation, V is reset. Inter-spike intervals become random even under constant input, matching in-vivo variability (CV of ISI ≈ 0.5–1.0).

Escape noise advantage: The hazard ρ(t) is differentiable with respect to parameters, enabling gradient-based learning rules for STDP. The stochastic neuron can be shown to perform gradient ascent on the log-likelihood of its observed spike train — a principled probabilistic interpretation of Hebbian learning.

Probabilistic STDP

In stochastic STDP, weight updates are expected values over the randomness in spike generation:

Expected STDP Update
E[Δw | pre spike at tpre] = ∫ STDP(t−tpre) · ρ(t) dt

This softens the sharp STDP window into a smooth gradient, producing more stable and noise-robust weight distributions than deterministic STDP.

Advantages

  • Captures trial-to-trial ISI variability — matches in-vivo recordings
  • Escape noise connects STDP to maximum likelihood / Bayesian inference
  • Smooth gradients from probabilistic framework — more stable STDP
  • Analytically tractable with the Fokker-Planck equation for population density

Limitations

  • Inherent timing jitter — STDP windows are probabilistically blurred
  • Stochastic simulations require many trials for reliable weight estimates
  • Hazard function parameters (ρ₀, δ_u) are hard to measure experimentally
