MODEL 03
Adaptive Exponential Integrate-and-Fire

AdEx adds a slow adaptation current w to the EIF model. A single neuron type controlled by four parameters (a, b, τ_w, and the EIF shape parameters) reproduces regular spiking, intrinsic bursting, fast spiking, transient spiking, and delayed spiking — the principal cortical cell types.

ODEs2
Spike PrecisionHigh
Dendritic EffectsNone
Computational CostLow
Biological FidelityMed-High

Governing Equations

Membrane & Adaptation
Cm · dV/dt = −gL(V − EL) + gL·δT·exp((V−VT)/δT) − w + I(t)
τw · dw/dt = a(V − EL) − w
SymbolDescriptionTypical
wAdaptation current — opposes depolarization; accumulates with firingpA
τwAdaptation time constant — controls how slowly w decays100–300 ms
aSubthreshold adaptation — couples w to V below threshold0–4 nS
bSpike-triggered adaptation increment — added to w at each spike0–100 pA
All EIF paramsCm, gL, EL, δT, VT as defined in EIF—

Reset Mechanism

Dual Reset
if V ≥ Vpeak : emit spike, V ← Vreset, w ← w + b

The key difference from EIF: the adaptation variable w also jumps at each spike by the amount b. This spike-triggered increment accumulates across bursts, progressively slowing firing and eventually halting it — the hallmark of adaptation.

Advantages

  • Reproduces all major cortical firing patterns with one model
  • Adaptation is physiologically crucial for STDP — changes effective ISIs
  • Low cost — two ODEs; easily integrated with 0.1 ms steps
  • Well-validated against intracellular recordings

Limitations

  • Six parameters to tune — harder to hand-set than LIF
  • Still a point neuron — no dendritic effects
  • No subthreshold oscillations or resonance
  • w variable has no direct biophysical analog (phenomenological)
