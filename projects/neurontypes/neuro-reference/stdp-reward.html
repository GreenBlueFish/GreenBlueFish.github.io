STDP 07
Reward-Modulated STDP (R-STDP)

A three-factor plasticity rule where local STDP-based eligibility traces are gated by a global reward signal — modelling the role of dopamine in corticostriatal plasticity. Bridges unsupervised Hebbian STDP and reinforcement learning, enabling a SNN to learn goal-directed behavior from delayed rewards.

Three-Factor Interpretation

The three factors:
1. Presynaptic activity — encoded in the pre trace x (as in classical STDP)
2. Postsynaptic activity — encoded in the post trace y (as in classical STDP)
3. Global reward signal r(t) — dopamine release or its absence

Without reward (r=0), synapses don't change even if eligible. When reward arrives, all recently eligible synapses undergo weight changes proportional to their eligibility. This solves the temporal credit assignment problem: the eligibility trace acts as a memory of which synapses contributed to the behavior that earned the reward.

Advantages

  • Bridges Hebbian STDP and reinforcement learning — both local and global signals
  • Biologically grounded in dopamine modulation of basal ganglia
  • Eligibility trace solves temporal credit assignment within one synapse
  • Enables sequence learning from delayed reward without backpropagation

Limitations

  • Global reward signal means all synapses update together — not layer-specific
  • Credit assignment across many layers remains unsolved
  • Eligibility trace τₑ must match reward delay, which varies across tasks
  • Requires external reward structure — not purely self-organized
