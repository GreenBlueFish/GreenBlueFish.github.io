STDP 02
Multiplicative (Weight-Dependent) STDP

A modification of classical STDP where the update magnitude scales with the distance from the weight bounds. Strong synapses are harder to potentiate and easier to depress; weak synapses the reverse. This produces stable, unimodal weight distributions without external normalization.

TypeUnsupervised
Traces2
CostVery Low
StabilityGood
Bio. PlausibilityHigh

Learning Rule

Soft-Bound Updates
Δw⁺ = A₊ · (wmax − w) · exp(−Δt/τ₊) if Δt > 0
Δw⁻ = −A₋ · (w − wmin) · exp(Δt/τ₋) if Δt < 0
SymbolDescriptionTypical
wmaxMaximum allowed synaptic weight1.0
wminMinimum allowed synaptic weight0.0
(wmax−w)Soft upper bound factor — potentiation shrinks as w approaches max0–1
(w−wmin)Soft lower bound factor — depression shrinks as w approaches min0–1
All other paramsA₊, A₋, τ₊, τ₋ as in classical STDP—

Why Soft Bounds Stabilize

Stability mechanism:
In additive STDP, every LTP update has the same amplitude A₊, regardless of how strong the synapse already is — runaway growth is possible.

In multiplicative STDP, when w = wmax: Δw⁺ = A₊ · (wmax − wmax) = 0 — potentiation stops automatically.
When w = wmin: Δw⁻ = −A₋ · (wmin − wmin) = 0 — depression stops automatically.

The weight distribution converges to a unimodal distribution centered between the bounds, not bimodal like additive STDP.

Advantages

  • Stable unimodal weight distributions without normalization
  • Implicit competition — strong synapses resist further growth
  • Same computational cost as classical STDP
  • Prevents weight saturation naturally

Limitations

  • Softer competition — less selectivity than additive rule
  • Harder to analyze theoretically (nonlinear weight dependence)
  • Reduced sensitivity of strong synapses — may limit learning speed
  • Biological evidence for the specific soft-bound form is limited
