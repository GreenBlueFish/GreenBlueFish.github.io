STDP 09
BCM Rule (Bienenstock-Cooper-Munro)

A rate-based plasticity rule with a sliding modification threshold θ_M that separates the LTP and LTD regimes. Firing above θ_M strengthens synapses; below weakens them. θ_M itself adapts to the neuron's recent activity. Underlies ocular dominance column formation and recoverable as the high-rate limit of triplet STDP.

Connection to STDP

In the high-rate limit, the expected weight change under classical pair-based STDP for Poisson inputs with rates rpre, rpost converges to:

STDP High-Rate Limit
E[Δw] ≈ (A₊·τ₊ − A₋·τ₋) · rpre · rpost

This is a pure Hebbian rate rule. The triplet STDP rule recovers BCM more precisely — the slow post-synaptic trace y₂ effectively encodes θ_M:

Triplet STDP → BCM: The y₂ trace in triplet STDP decays with τ_{y2} ≈ 40 ms, integrating recent postsynaptic spiking. When y₂ is large (high post rate), the A₃⁺ term amplifies LTP — equivalent to operating above θ_M. When y₂ is small (low post rate), the A₂⁻ term dominates, giving LTD — equivalent to below-threshold BCM. The BCM threshold θ_M corresponds to the equilibrium point of y₂.

Advantages

  • Sliding threshold provides intrinsic homeostasis — prevents runaway
  • Mathematically elegant — single φ function covers LTP and LTD
  • Experimentally validated in visual cortex plasticity
  • Connected to triplet STDP — provides rate-level interpretation

Limitations

  • Rate-based — loses spike-timing information entirely
  • θ_M adaptation is very slow (hours) — not appropriate for fast learning
  • BCM does not predict plasticity from individual spike pairs
  • Biological substrate for the sliding threshold is not fully identified
