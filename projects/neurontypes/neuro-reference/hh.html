MODEL 05
Hodgkin-Huxley Model

The biophysical gold standard, derived from voltage-clamp experiments on the squid giant axon by Hodgkin and Huxley in 1952. Four coupled ODEs govern membrane voltage and three ionic gating variables. The spike shape, after-hyperpolarization, and refractory period all emerge naturally from the dynamics — no explicit reset is needed.

ODEs4+
Spike PrecisionRealistic
Dendritic EffectsPartial
Computational CostHigh

Reset Mechanism

There is no explicit reset. The spike arises organically:

Spike sequence:
1. Depolarization opens m gates fast (Na⁺ in) → regenerative upstroke
2. h gates inactivate (blocking Na⁺) + n gates open slow (K⁺ out) → repolarization
3. n remains elevated after spike → after-hyperpolarization (AHP)
4. h recovers slowly → absolute refractory period while h is inactivated

Relevance to STDP

The HH model is essential when studying STDP mechanisms that depend on realistic voltage trajectories:

STDP MechanismWhy HH Matters
NMDA-dependent STDPNMDA conductance depends on V nonlinearly — HH gives realistic V waveforms
Backpropagating AP (bAP)bAP amplitude/shape depends on Na⁺ channel kinetics in dendrites
Calcium-based plasticityCa²⁺ influx through NMDA and VGCC depends on exact V trajectory
AHP effects on STDPAHP sets next spike timing; HH AHP is realistic; LIF AHP is artificial

Advantages

  • Gold standard — highest biological fidelity of any single-compartment model
  • Spike shape, AHP, and refractoriness emerge naturally
  • Enables mechanistic study of NMDA, Ca²⁺, and channel-level STDP
  • Extensible — add channels (Ca²⁺, IA, etc.) as extra ODEs

Limitations

  • High cost — 4 ODEs per compartment, Δt must be ≈ 0.01 ms
  • Fitting to non-squid neurons requires re-deriving α/β functions
  • Large network simulations infeasible without simplification
  • Rate functions are empirical — not universal across species
